# Rims investigation (spoke rims that turn with speed, and spinner rims)

Written 14-Oct for the owner by an investigation agent; saved 15-Oct from the agent's transcript, because it had not been saved anywhere. The throwaway spike is on branch `claude/rims-spike` (commit 5b11bec, NOT for main). Nothing here is built on main.

RIMS INVESTIGATION REPORT (investigation only; nothing pushed, no game code changed on any shared branch)

VERDICT
1. Feasible: yes, and a lot of the plumbing already exists. A wheel-cosmetic system is already on main (v1.1.132): render/wheelcosmetic.js, cosmetics.js, cosmeticsui.js. It has 12 "wheel sets" (tyre ring + hub colour), a Cosmetics equip screen, an ownership registry and unlockall.js integration. Rims should be the next item in that system, not a new one.
2. Difficulty: medium. A static spoke rim on every car took me about 40 lines. Spinning is also easy. The costs are (a) no car has speed-driven wheels today, (b) no per-car wheel table exists (wheels are re-derived from geometry), and (c) the odd cars.
3. Biggest risk: wheels are found by geometry heuristics (findHubs), not data, so odd cars (monowheel, tracks, hovercraft, boats, tractor, 3-wheelers) need an opt-out or an override table. Also spokes that sit on the body-side suspension heuristics (the cause of the "bounced with the car" bug). Wagon-wheel aliasing is real but easy to mitigate.
4. Rims can stay render-only, outside the replay hash. I read vite.config.js:23-37 (logicFiles walks only src/delivery/*.js minus NOT_RULES and their imports; render/* is not walked unless a rules module imports it) and cosmetics.js:11-15 says the same. Keep rim code in render/ and import nothing rules-side.
5. Recommended first slice: one spoke rim, spinning in the garage and the Cosmetics close-up on the standard cars, using the existing wheel-set machinery. About 1 to 2 days, roughly 200 lines. Already prototyped as a throwaway (see 6).

1. HOW WHEELS ARE BUILT AND ANIMATED TODAY
- Models are builders in render/models.js (MODELS map; line 1 header says userData.animate(t) is called every frame the model is drawn). Further cars are in modelsOrig*.js, ideaModels*.js and the batch files. Most models use a local wheel() helper that returns [tyre, hub]: a CylinderGeometry tyre plus a smaller hub cylinder, separate meshes with separate materials (models.js:19-28). The ideaModels7 "alloys" helper adds spokes and a cap, merged.
- Wheel spin is TIME-based, not speed-based. Only about 4 models spin wheels at all: models.js:180, 219 and 333 (`for (w of wheels) w.rotation.x = t*30`) and one in ideaModels3 (the Monowheel). t is wall-clock: items.js:535 calls `custom.userData.animate(performance.now()/1000)` for the player car and cars.js:266 does the same for traffic. garage.js:608 and showroomview.js:214 do the same. So those wheels turn at a fixed 30 rad/s even when the car is stopped, and no model reads Player.speed. Every other car's wheels never rotate. This is the reason for the "did not animate while driving" deletions.
- Standard box traffic cars: wheels are 4 meshes with shared geometry (cars.js:24, 47). They are not rotated.
- Tyre and rim are separate meshes in most models. Per-car wheel data does not exist: the wheel table is derived. wheelcosmetic.js findHubs (lines 104-138) finds tyres as non-Box discs on their side touching the floor, then finds hubs as meshes on the axle. carsuspension.js prepareSuspension (lines 33-94) uses the same idea, or honours model.userData.planted. Per tyre, findHubs yields centre, axleX, radius, half-width and which side (info[]), which is exactly what a rim attach needs.
- Steering: no model steers its wheels (no front-wheel yaw found).
- Suspension: carsuspension.js plants the wheels on the floor and moves the body. It treats any mesh touching the floor, shaped like a disc, as a wheel, plus everything inside its box. It is used only for the showroom landing bounce (carbounce.js), not in-game.
- Dents: render/dents.js leaves wheels alone (header line 13), and skips meshes tagged userData.wheelRing (dents.js:109) and materials tagged wheelHub (dents.js:154). Any new rim mesh must carry these two tags.
- Instancing: nothing in cars is an InstancedMesh (findHubs skips them anyway). The blocker for per-wheel rotation is MERGING, not instancing: see the next item.
- Model editor and parts helper: modelpatch*.js and modeleditorshim.js address parts by index; a rim added after the model is built (appended last, like the Legendary wreath in legenddecal.js) does not disturb part ids.

WHY THE TRIMS WERE DELETED (commits f257e0e, 792b3c5, on origin/claude/great-hamilton-lhm0us)
- ideaModels7.js alloys() built the spokes and caps of all four wheels into ONE merged mesh (`merged(group, spokeMaterial, parts)`). That mesh is not a disc touching the floor, so carsuspension treats it as body (sprung): in the showroom it bounced with the body while the tyres stayed planted. The merged mesh also hangs off the model group, not off any tyre, so nothing could rotate it per wheel. The fix was to add a `trims=false` parameter and delete them for the Sixteen-Cylinder Coupe, Knife-Edge Supercar and Wind-Song Racer. Lesson: rim geometry must be a CHILD OF THE TYRE (as wheelcosmetic.js's sidewall rings already are), never merged across wheels.

3. FEASIBILITY AND DESIGN
- Where the code sits: a new render/rims.js (or an extension of wheelcosmetic.js), called from the same dressWheels hook so every call site already works. It reuses findHubs' info[] (tyre, centre, axleX, r, half, outer, toTyre). Rim meshes are children of the tyre, tagged wheelRing (so dents and suspension skip them) and wheelHub material.
- (A) Speed-driven rotation. The tyre mesh is the natural thing to rotate, but it also carries the tread, so rotate only the rim mesh around the axle (the spike does this). Speed source: Player.speed (player.js:74) is the logic speed; ground speed in the world is scaled by CONFIG.trueSpeed (config.js:181, track.js:593 pathScale: bends make the path longer or shorter), so true m/s along the ground is NOT Player.speed exactly. I did not verify the exact conversion; the angular rate is about speed / radius, and for a cosmetic Player.speed * pathScale is close enough. Angle must be integrated per frame (angle += speed/r * dt), never computed from time. Direction: sign by the wheel's side because the rim is built facing outward. NOT verified in game: no speed-driven rim was run.
- Garage / shop / idle: the showroom and garage have speed 0. A speed-driven rim needs a fake idle: either a slow constant "show" rate when not in game, or a looped 0 to cruise ramp. (B) The spinner just turns at a fixed rate everywhere, which is why it suits the showroom.
- Per-car data: no wheel table exists. Derivation works for ordinary cars (spike verified on Pick-Up and Commuter, see 6). Cars that need an opt-out or special handling (read from code, not rendered): Monowheel (one big hoop, noBounce flag, carriage hangs from rollers), Hovercraft (no wheels), Snowcat/Half-Track/Tank (tracks), Jetboat/Trawler/boats (no wheels), Farm Tractor (huge rear wheels under mudguards), the three- and one-wheelers (Wedge Microcar, Tuk-Tuk, Rickshaw), Moon Rover (wire wheels), Legendary cars (skip by design), 8x8 and Tipper (many axles; findHubs handles them if each tyre has a hub). The Cosmetics screen should tell the player when the car has no rimmable wheels. Suggest a small override table: `RIM_SKIP` plus an optional `userData.rims` list that a model can set (as userData.planted already works).
- Other views: Ghost power-up: rim meshes need the solidMat handling that rings have (wheelcosmetic.js:204). Night/lamps: the ring material has some emissive so it reads in shade; reuse. The Legendary decal and underglow already have ghostify caveats (259).

4. COST
- Draw calls: one mesh per wheel face today (rings do this); a 4-wheeler with an outer face each is 4 extra meshes (8 if both faces of a centre wheel). Share one geometry and one material across all wheels and cars (as ringGeo/ringMat do). Cars have a few hundred to about 2,000 triangles, so a 5-spoke rim is a +~100 to +300 triangle bump over 4 wheels.
- Per frame: one quaternion update per rim mesh; negligible. Do not rely on matrixAutoUpdate off.
- Mobile (Mi Pad): extra draw calls matter more than triangles. Keep one material, one geometry per design, and only dress the player car (traffic stays undressed). Merging all rims of one car into one mesh is wrong (that was the bug).
- Textures: not needed for the designs; geometry is cleaner and spins correctly. A canvas texture (<=256x256, drawn in code) would suit "mesh" or fine-spoke styles that are too thin for geometry, on one disc per wheel (32 tris). Hybrid is possible.
- Sample designs, rough triangles per rim face (box-spoke geometry = 12 tris per spoke, plus centre cap about 24): (A) speed-driven: 5-spoke about 85, 10-spoke twin about 150, turbine (8 angled blades, flat quads) about 40, split-star (5 forked spokes) about 140, mesh (canvas disc, 32 tris). (B) Spinner: bladed (3 bars) about 60, twin-blade about 40, disc with offset weight about 40, "floating" hub that spins separately from the wheel about 60.

5. RISKS
- Wagon-wheel aliasing: a 5-spoke at a high rate shows stroboscopic reverse spin when the per-frame angle approaches or exceeds half the spoke spacing (36 degrees for 5 spokes). At 60 fps that is about 10 rev/s; at a wheel radius of 0.35 m that is about 22 m/s, which the game exceeds. Mitigation: cap the angular rate to under half the spoke gap per frame, then crossfade to a plain blurred disc (a translucent 16-sided disc, ~16 tris) above a threshold speed. Spinners at a fixed slow rate avoid the issue by design. Not tested.
- Dents/damage: new meshes must carry userData.wheelRing and wheelHub (already respected by dents.js:109,154 and carsuspension.js:47,92). The "bounce" bug (above) came from merging across wheels and parenting to the group.
- Z-fighting with brake discs and hubcaps: wheelcosmetic.js uses RING_GAP = 5 mm over the 4 mm threshold of the z-fight detector (render/zfight.js). The spike used 10 mm. Cars with a hub standing proud of the tyre may poke through a flat spoke face: check per car. Wheels that already carry spokes or trim (do not stack a second set): ideaModels7.js alloys() users (Sixteen-Cylinder Coupe, Knife-Edge Supercar, Wind-Song Racer, which now have none after the deletions, plus any other alloys() call that still has trims), modelsOrig1b/3/4 hubs, Legendary gold hub caps (skipped), Moon Rover wire wheels. I did not grep for every model with spokes.
- Unseen: how the car looks from all sides with rims; the hub that remains behind a spoke rim (spoke rims on top of a stock hub looked fine on the Commuter, a bit lumpy on the Pick-Up).
