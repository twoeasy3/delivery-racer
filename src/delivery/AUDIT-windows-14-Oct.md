# Window audit, 14-Oct (2026-10-14)

Read-only audit for the owner (14-Oct: "get an agent to audit these issues for all cars"): the two recurring window faults, how
widespread they are. Taken at `origin/main` v1.1.130. **Nothing here was looked at in a browser**: every number comes from the
geometry of the models as the game builds them (`render/showcar.js makeShowCar` through a headless Vite loader; `MODELS[...]` for the
traffic kinds). The numbers are regenerated by `node scripts/audit-windows.mjs` (`--write` rewrites `src/delivery/windows-audit.json`,
`--md` prints the table below, `--png <dir> <id>` draws a car's side view with the contour and the windows, for looking). I read about
25 of those pictures while building the script; the rest is unlooked.

The two faults (the owner's words, 14-Oct):

1. **Contour.** "The pink line (chassis contour) should dictate the purple line (window contour)": a window's top, front and rear edges
   run parallel to the body's own side profile at one constant inset (pillar width); not a free-standing dome, arch or quad.
2. **Heights.** "Almost every car's windscreen is higher than the side window": the windscreen's top edge and the side window's top edge are
   at the same height at the A-pillar; windscreen side edge and side-window front edge are one pillar of constant width; the rear glass
   meets the side window the same way at the C-pillar.

## 2. Method and accuracy

**Built** as the game builds them (271 vehicles, the list of AUDIT-plates-14-Oct.md). Per vehicle every non-instanced visible mesh
is taken in car-local space (+z front, +x left).

**Glass detection (rule and misses).** A lit (not MeshBasic) material counts as glass when it is dark and blue-ish (`b - r >= 12`, `b >= g`,
no channel above 0x70: the shared `GLASS` 0x1c2330, the ideas' 0x232a35, 0x2a3340, 0x1d2531, 0x1a2230, 0x33465c and so on) or is
transparent and untextured. The older boxy cars use a near-black "tint" (0x0d0f14) that cannot be told from black trim by colour:
it counts as glass only for a car with no navy glass at all (this finds the Limo, the traffic Police, Ambulance, Driveby; it is the
least certain rule). Not glass: the body's own paint (even when dark navy), vertex-coloured materials, cylinders / circles / tori in a glass colour (wheel rims and
lamp lenses), textured decals (road dust). Spheres, lathes and capsules in glass colour are listed as **domes**. Misses I know of: a window drawn as body
paint or baked into a vertex colour (none found, but not excluded: the Sport has side glass and no screen mesh at all, which I took as real); glass in a colour
outside the rule (a glass material coloured like trim would be missed; all 24 glassless vehicles were checked by eye in the source or the lists, they are open
or glassless).

**Pieces.** Glass triangles are split into edge-connected pieces (neighbouring normals within 25 degrees); a smoothly curved piece (normal spread over
20 degrees, over 0.15 m2) is a canopy; others are classed by their outward normal: side window (|nx| >= 0.55, left = +x), windscreen
(nz >= 0.3), rear glass (nz <= -0.3), roof glass (ignored). A thin glass box counts once (its outer face); a raked glass slab's edge faces are not windows;
a screen is the outermost glass facing the front; lamp lenses, grille slats and badges are dropped (a window must share the greenhouse's height band
with the largest window, and lie near the roofline and the cabin). Glass 15 cm or more clear of every body part is listed as `floating` and left out (the
Pick-Up has one such sliver above its cab). Domed or bevelled-solid glass that welds into one piece is a canopy, not panels.

**Body side profile (the "pink line").** The silhouette of the body seen along x: every non-glass, non-glowing, non-tyre triangle rasterised on a 1 cm grid
in (z, y), thin parts (under 4 cm: aerials, stalks) removed by an opening. The body mesh's own silhouette (`userData.body`) is used when it holds at least 80
percent of the glass vertices within 3 cm, otherwise every body part (this is the case for the older cars, whose cabin is separate boxes: their "roofline" then
includes roof slabs, racks and stacks, so their inset numbers are an upper bound). Accuracy: about 1 cm in position, which is the grid; the opening can round an
acute corner by about 1.4 cm. The profile is the true extrusion outline for the newer cars (the Wedge Microcar's silhouette matches its source outline) and a
union of boxes for the older ones. Supers are measured with the kit's added parts hidden.

**Numbers per car.** For each side window: the hull of its vertices in (z, y); the top-front corner TF is the front-most vertex of its upper half, TR the rear-most.
- *Heights (fault 2):* windscreen top y minus the front-most side window's TF y (A-pillar); rear glass top y minus the rear-most window's TR y (C-pillar). Sign: plus means the screen is higher.
- *Pillar width:* the 3-D distance between the screen's outer side edge and the window's front (rear) edge at equal heights, ten samples over their common height range; *variation* is max minus min. (This differs from the E5 check's side-view width, section 6.) Also the angle between the two edges in the side view.
- *Top-edge angle:* fitted slope of the window's top edge against the fitted slope of the body's roofline over the same z span, degrees.
- *Inset variation:* max minus min of (roofline height minus window top height) over the top edge, cm. Also in the json: the distance of the top edge and of the front / rear edges (above the bonnet line) to the nearest contour point.
- *Symmetry:* largest left/right difference of corner positions; 99 marks a different window count left and right.
- *Shape:* quad, polygon (n vertices) or curved/canopy.

Verdict thresholds (`TOL` in the script; pass up to the first, marginal up to the second, fail above): height 2 / 4 cm; top-edge angle 1.5 / 3 degrees; inset variation 2 / 4 cm;
pillar variation 2 / 4 cm; left-right 1 / 3 cm. **Fault 2 is the worse of the A and C height differences; fault 1 is the worst of angle, inset variation and pillar variation, and
fails outright for a curved window or a canopy.** These are my choices, not the owner's (section 7).

**Cross-checks.** (a) Heights agree with the other agent's check (section 6) to 0.1 cm on the idea cars I compared (Slantback 7.4 and 8.3, Glass-Hatch 9.6, Wedge 3.3, Kei Van 5.0, Hill-Climb 4.7).
(b) The Wedge Microcar's 5 degree angle is its source (roof rising 6 cm over 0.7 m behind a flat window top). (c) Supers equal their bases. (d) I looked at the pictures of the Commuter, Wedge Microcar, Sprint Hatch, Pixel EV, Food Truck, Pick-Up, Dampervan, Hot Hatch, Slantback, Glass-Hatch, Herald, Armoured Limo and others; the
numbers matched what is drawn, and the glassless / floating / lamp-lens misreads I found were fixed in the script before this table.

**What I could not measure well.** (1) The older boxy cars: their glass is a full-length glass block with paint pillars in front, so the "window" is the whole glasshouse side and
the pillars (paint boxes) are not recovered; their inset and pillar numbers (up to 100 cm) are mostly roof-slab overhang and a glass slab proud of the cabin, i.e. real but coarse. (2) Hinged doors and
open roofs: the models are measured as built, not animated. (3) Glass done as vertex colour or texture would be missed. (4) Concave windows are measured by their convex hull.
(5) Whether a canopy is a deliberate bubble top. (6) Nothing is looked at in the game's own camera.

## 5. What a reusable fix looks like

A helper that takes the body's own outline (the `[z, y]` points handed to `extrudeSide`) and **insets it**, so every glass edge comes from the same line:
1. Pick the glasshouse part of the outline: from the cowl point (foot of the A-pillar) over the A-pillar, the roof, the C-pillar to the boot or the tail foot (an index range or two named points).
2. Offset that open polyline inward by a pillar width (roof inset, A-pillar inset, C-pillar inset: three numbers, defaults 0.07 to 0.09 m), mitred, then close it with a belt line at `belt` y. That polygon is the **side window**, flat on the flank (narrowed by the car's taper as `sideGlass` does today); cut it with B-pillars where wanted (a z range per window).
3. Build the **windscreen and rear glass from the same offset line**: the screen's top edge is the inset roof line's point at the A-pillar corner (so the same y as the side window's top-front corner by construction), its side edges follow the A-pillar's offset line, its width at height y is the body's width there minus the pillar width.
4. One pillar width, one place: the screen's side edge and the window's front edge are the two edges of the strip of body between them, so the pillar width is constant by construction.

The other agent's `cabinGlass` / `glassFromProfile` / `screenGlassFromProfile` / `insetOutline` in `ideaModelsDkit.js` on `claude/ideas-batch-e5` is exactly this (I did not touch or duplicate it; I only read it). For the 70 vehicles
built from glass boxes or `curved()` canopies there is no outline to inset: the helper needs a second entry that takes the cabin's box-and-slab layout (a glasshouse profile given as points) or the models need an outline for the
greenhouse first. Models with a canopy by design (section 7) need a decision, not a helper.

## 7. Questions for the owner

1. **Tolerance:** how many cm of mismatch is acceptable between the screen's top and the side window's top (this audit passes 2 cm, marginal to 4; the other agent's check uses 1.5)? And the top-edge angle (1.5 degrees pass, 3 marginal)?
2. **Older cars:** fix the 147 older vehicles (garage, filler, Bespoke, Legendary and the traffic twins) too, or only the cars built today and new ones? Fixing a garage car fixes its Super, Legendary and traffic twin for free (the base model is the one file).
3. **Canopies and domes (18 vehicles + 6 mixed):** is the Rotary, EV Saloon, Classic GT, Fishbowl, Bubble, UFO and so on a deliberate bubble top (no pillars at all), or should they get a pillar frame too?
4. **Glass blocks with paint pillars** (the older boxy cars: Limo, Hot Hatch, Rally, Muscle, buses): is a full-length dark block under painted pillars acceptable as "windows", or should each window be its own pane inset from the pillars?
5. **Windscreen-only open cars** (28): nothing to match; leave them?
6. **Fault 1's "constant inset":** measured from the roof and the pillars at one width each: should the roof inset and the pillar inset be allowed to differ (e.g. thin roof rail, wider pillar)? The audit allows any constant on each edge.
7. The **Sport has no windscreen or rear glass** and the **Wedge, Quad Turbo, Knife-Edge and Motorhome have different window counts left and right**: bugs, or deliberate?
