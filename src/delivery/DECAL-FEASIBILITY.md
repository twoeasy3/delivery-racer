# Flank decals: a feasibility check (15-Oct, CHECKLIST 383)

The owner asked to choose 6 very different cars and check whether their side profiles can be tagged for a decal cosmetic.
A decal cosmetic is a design the player picks (a stripe, a race number, flames, a roundel) stuck on the car's flanks, like
the plate and prop cosmetics.

This is a prototype on branch `claude/decal-feasibility`: it is not merged, has no version bump and is OFF for players.

**Verdict: GO-IF** (section 9). The search finds usable zones automatically. A projected mesh costs one draw call and 8 to
132 triangles a car, and it looks right on all six cars. The open points are what the owner wants done about cars with
little paint, dents, and colour contrast.

## 1. The six cars

| car (id, model) | why it was chosen | easy or hard |
|---|---|---|
| Post Van (`postvan`) | Flat-sided box van: large slab flanks. | Easy: 56% of the side view is clean paint and every zone is flat to 0 mm. The van's own envelope graphic and rub strip are left alone. |
| People's Bug (`bug`) | The round-bodied car. | Easier than expected: the model's tub side is flat (a 1.12 x 0.60 m door at 0 mm). The roundness is in the spherical fenders (normals spread 22 to 29 degrees, 47 mm off a plane), where a wrapped decal takes 68 triangles. |
| Wedge Supercar (`wedge`) | Low and thin-flanked, with big arches and box-built stepped panels (3.5 cm steps). | Hard: wheels cover 27% of the side view, and the steps cut the flank into small zones (largest roundel 0.36 m, stripe 0.96 m). |
| Flatbed Truck (`flatbed`) | A multi-part lorry: a separate cab and a load bed whose sides are timber, not paint. | Medium: only the cab door and front wing are paint (24% clean). The bed takes nothing unless the owner wants decals on boards. |
| City Bus (`bus`, model `citybus`) | A long vehicle (11 m), mostly glass, with doors on one side only. | Easy for size (a stripe up to 10.96 m), hard for symmetry: the left and right clean areas agree at only 0.90 IoU (intersection over union). The decal is laid on the zones both flanks share. |
| Woody Wagon (`woody`) | Odd geometry: timber flanks over a painted body. | Hard: the timber is trim, so only the front wing and the strip by the door are paint (17% clean, roundel 0.48 m). |

There is no separate trailer or tanker among the player cars. The multi-part case is the Flatbed (cab, bed, load). The
Cement Mixer (`mixer`, cab plus drum) was in the whole-roster survey (section 7: mirror IoU 0.87).

## 2. The tag (zone) format

The table is `src/delivery/render/decalzonestable.js`. It is generated by `node scripts/decal-zones.mjs --write` and
keyed by the car's MODEL, like the plate table:

```js
postvan: { L: [['door', -1.03, 1.05, 0.38, 0.78, 0, 0], ['quarter', -2.07, -1.27, 0.94, 1.66, 0, 0], ...], R: 'L' },
citybus: { L: [...], R: [...] },   // the right differs (its doors): its own rows
```

- **A row** is `[id, z0, z1, y0, y1, flat mm, curve deg]`, in car-local metres (z along the car with the nose at +z, y up
  from the road). It is a rectangle of the side view.
- **id**: `door` (between the wheels), `quarter` (over or behind the rear wheels), `fender` (over or ahead of the front
  wheels), or `flank` (a zone longer than 2.6 m between the wheels). `bed` is reserved.
- **side**: `L`, and `R: 'L'` when the right flank is the same within 3 cm. That covers 133 of the 161 models.
- **surface**: always the body's PAINT (the body mesh, or any part in the body's paint material), facing out sideways.
- **excluded**: everything that is not paint: wheels, glass, lamps, trim (bumpers, chrome, rub strips, timber, handles,
  plates) and the car's own see-through graphics (rust, chequers).
- **max size**: the zone's rectangle. A design is fitted inside it (a roundel at most `min(w, h)`).
- **mirror pairing**: `pairedZones(key)` cuts each left zone to the right zones it overlaps, so one livery reads the same
  on both flanks.
- A hand row from the model editor would carry `by: 'editor'` and `prev`, as plates and props do. This is not built.

**How tags are made: (C), both.** The central search proposes the zones (built: section 3). The model editor's Placements
tab would edit them and protect the edits (`by: 'editor'`, as for plates and props). The editor part is not built.

## 3. The search, and its numbers

`scripts/decal-zones.mjs` holds the CLI, the audit, and the decal and dent measurements. `scripts/decal-zones-lib.mjs`
holds the search. The ray rule they share is `render/flankdecal.js` (`flankView`, `flankHit`), the same code the game's
decal uses. The search works like this:

1. Rays are cast along x every 4 cm over each flank's side view. Each ray's first hit is classed as paint (facing within
   about 45 degrees of sideways), curve, glass, wheel, lamp, trim, graphic or air.
2. A 4 cm cell is CLEAN when its four corners are paint and the surface between them is smooth. Smooth means no more
   than 5 cm apart in x, and moving as the normals predict within 6 mm, so a ledge, a panel gap or a box-built car's
   step is an edge.
3. The largest clean rectangles are taken greedily: up to 4 a side, each at least 0.30 x 0.15 m, with an aspect ratio of
   12 or less. A zone whose normals spread more than 35 degrees is dropped (a roof shoulder).

The full audit is in `/tmp/decal-zones/audit.md`, with a copy in the scratchpad folder.

**A "door roundel + stripe" decal** (a 0.5 m roundel and a 1.5 m stripe) fits whole on the Post Van, the Bug, the Bus
(roundel up to 1 m) and the Flatbed's cab. The Woody takes a 0.48 m roundel and a 1.4 m stripe. The Wedge takes 0.36 m and

**Known limits:**

- The 4 cm grid misses slivers narrower than 4 cm: the Wedge's stripe edges onto its black side intake.
- A zone is axis-aligned, so a sloping waist line is followed only in steps.

## 4. Rendering, and its measured cost

`render/flankdecal.js` lays the decal by the legenddecal technique with one change: the lattice is SIMPLIFIED.

- **How it is laid.** Rays find the paint under each piece's rectangle on a lattice about 4 cm apart. Then the coarsest
  power-of-two lattice whose faces stay within 2 mm of every measured point is kept. A flat van door is 1 quad. The Bug's spherical
  fender takes about 15 quads a side (68 triangles for the whole decal, both flanks). Each point stands 4 mm out along its normal, with a polygon offset of -4 and no depth
  write. Where something covers the paint, a point's vertex alpha is 0, so the edge is clean (alpha test 0.5).
- **One mesh a car.** Both flanks and both pieces go in one mesh in one shared material: **one draw call a car**. It is
  parented where the body is, so it rides the suspension.
- **Number direction.** The roundel's texture runs right to left on the right flank, so its number reads correctly from
  each side. The stripe runs nose-ward on both sides. `.decal-zones-check` asserts both.
- **The picture.** One 256 x 256 canvas (256 KB, about 341 KB with mipmaps) drawn in code with flat shapes: a white disc,
  a black ring and a number; a near-black band between two white pinstripes. It is shared by every car. There are no
  image files.
- **The flag.** `?decaltest=1` turns it on, `=zones` shows the zone overlay, `=both` shows both, and `=curve` puts the
  roundel on the most curved zone. `&decallift=<mm>` and `&decalnum=<n>` set the lift and the number. The hook is one call
  in `render/showcar.js dressCosmetics`, so the garage, the showroom and the game all wear it. Without `?decaltest`
  nothing is added: `.decal-zones-check` proves the six models are untouched. It is render-only and not in the replay
  fingerprint.
- **Cost a car**, measured (`decal-zones.mjs`, and the review page counting `renderer.info`):

| car | triangles | rays at dressing | build time (node, this machine) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Post Van | 8 | 1228 | 13 to 38 ms |
| Bug | 8 (68 on the curved fender) | 908 | 60 to 75 ms |
| Wedge | 8 | 908 | 10 to 14 ms |
| Flatbed | 132 (the stripe crosses an edge: the fine lattice is kept) | 1172 | 26 to 57 ms |
| Bus | 8 | 1748 | 12 to 21 ms |
| Woody | 8 | 748 | 75 to 120 ms |

Draw calls are +1 a car in every view counted. The build time is the one real cost: the rays run brute-force with no
BVH (bounding-volume hierarchy) while the car is dressed. Fixes: cache the wrapped geometry per model and layout, or bake
the lattice points into the table, which adds bytes.

**The texture alternative (painting into the body's material) is not feasible.** Evidence from the six models:

- None of them has a textured paint material.
- Their UVs are only three.js's defaults. Every face of a box maps the whole texture (the Post Van's body: 12 of its 12
  triangles span more than a quarter of the texture), and extrudes are mapped in world units.
- The paint is spread over 1 to 13 meshes a car.
- Its colour is set at runtime: Good and Evil, the scorch, and the Legendary black.

A texture decal would need every one of the 161 models UV-unwrapped by hand.

## 5. What dents, recolour and the blackout do

**Dents.** The decal is a large non-paint part, so `render/dents.js` crumples it vertex by vertex with the same shove as
the paint. It bends and moves with the panel, and its triangles grow from 8 to 45 to 230 at damage. `forgetDents` is
called after dressing, so a model measured earlier is measured again with the decal on.

Measured heights over the paint under each vertex (mm):

| car | clean | 20% | 60% | 100% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Post Van | 4 | 4..13 | 4..15 | 4..13 |
| Bus | 4 | -0.7..7.5 | 4..4.4 | 4..7.7 |
| Wedge | 4 | 4..13 | 3 of 53 vertices under paint | follows |
| Bug | 4 | 4 | 12 under paint | 15 under paint |
| Flatbed | 4 | 11 under paint | 4 under paint | 24 under paint |
| Woody | 4 | 4 | 2 under paint | 20 under paint |

**Seen** (`bug-d60`, `flatbed-d60`, `woody-d100`): the decal follows the dents, but folded shards of other crumpled paint
cover parts of it. A nick out of the Bug's and the Flatbed's roundel at 60%; at 100% a large shard over the Woody's
roundel. It reads as damage. It never floats off.

I tried cutting the decal and the paint under it finer (1.5 mm tolerance in dents.js). It made no visible difference,
because the cause is folded paint, not the lattice. That change was reverted: dents.js is untouched. Options:
(a) accept it; (b) hide the decal from damage stage 3 or 4 (one line); (c) "scuff" it.

**Recolour (Good / Evil).** The decal's colours are fixed, and the paint changes under it.

- **Seen:** the near-black stripe and the roundel's ring vanish on the Evil Bug's black paint (`bug-evil`).
- **Seen:** the first draft's red band vanished on the red Post Van.
- The yellow Evil Post Van is fine (`postvan-evil`).

A real design needs contrast built in (an outline or two tones) or an ink picked against the paint. Picking an ink costs
one material a car, with no extra draw call. The scorch darkens the paint but not the decal, which is not handled.

**Blackout.** The decal is hidden (`userData.blackout: 'hide'`, the same rule as the Legendary wreath). Seen in
`postvan-blackout`. The `.decal-zones-check` asserts it.

**Z-fighting and gaps at 2 to 6 mm.** Seen in grazing close-ups of the Bug (`bug-close-2mm`, `bug-close-6mm`): no
z-fighting, and no visible gap at either lift. 4 mm is the default.

**Curved bodies.** Seen in `bug-curve`: the roundel wraps the spherical fender with 68 triangles, at most 2 mm off the
surface.

Estimate:

| work | agent-days |
|---|---|
| Review sheets for all 161 models (as the prop audit did), with hand rows where needed | 1 to 1.5 |
| The cosmetic itself: designs, a slot, the per-car loadout, the Cosmetics screen, colours and contrast, the dents rule, caching the build, checks | 2 to 3 |

**Data size.** The table is 1780 bytes for 6 models (809 gzipped); the rows alone are 1217 B (421 gzipped). At about
200 B a model, all 161 come to about 33 KB raw and about 10 KB gzipped. Rounding to centimetres and dropping the
flat / curve columns about halves that. The canvas adds 256 KB of GPU memory per distinct design, and no download.

## 9. Recommendation: GO-IF

**GO** for paint-only, mesh-projected flank decals with zones from the central search plus editor-protected hand rows,
**IF** the owner accepts:

1. **Cars with little paint get little or no decal.** The Woody's timber, the Flatbed's bed boards and box-built stepped
   cars like the Wedge take only small zones, and about 30 models have no room for a 0.35 m roundel. The alternative,
   decals over trim and timber, is possible but would look stuck on.
2. **Decals crumple with the panels.** Shards of folded paint cover parts of them at heavy damage. The alternative is to
   hide them from 60% or 80% damage.
3. **Contrast is part of each design** (outlined or two-tone), not left to the paint.

**Risks:**

- **Build time.** 10 to 120 ms a car while it is dressed, which is a hitch in the menus: cache or bake.
- **Models redrawn later.** Their zones go stale: a check (search against the table) has to catch it.
- **Unequal flanks** (9 models): the paired-zone rule handles them, but a design is then small on one side.
- **Scorch.** The decal does not darken with the paint.

**Questions for the owner:**

1. Where does a design go: fixed per design (a roundel on the door, a stripe full length), or the player picks a zone?
2. Paint only, or over timber, boards and trim too?
3. Dents: crumple with the car, or peel off at heavy damage?
4. Colours: fixed per design, chosen by the player, or picked automatically against the paint?
5. Real size everywhere (a 0.55 m roundel on a bus too), or scaled with the vehicle?
6. Should the Legendary cars wear decals besides the wreath? And the player's number: digits only (filtered like the plate
   text)?
